How We Treat Each Other — The Ember House Community Standard
The Ember House — Version 1.0 — 2026 — Orangeville–Caledon–Amaranth, Ontario

The Ember House is not just a venue. It is a community — one built by and for people who have chosen to live and love in ways that require courage, honesty, and mutual care. Every person who walks through this door brings that with them.

Our Standards of Practice cover the rules. Our Guest Waiver covers the legal framework. This document is something different. This is about how we show up for each other — the tone we set, the warmth we extend, and the kind of space we build together every time we gather here.

The Ember House works because everyone in it takes responsibility not just for their own experience, but for the experience of everyone around them. That is the standard we hold ourselves to. We ask you to hold it too.

Section 01
The Tone We Strive For

The Ember House is a warm, welcoming, and intentional space. It is not a nightclub, a dungeon club, or a party venue. It is a private home that has been opened to a community. We ask that you feel that and honour it.

Warm, Not Performative
We are here to connect, explore, and enjoy each other's company — not to perform for an audience. Leave your ego at the door. Be genuinely curious about the people around you. Ask questions. Listen. The best conversations at The Ember House happen between strangers who are really paying attention to each other.
Relaxed, Not Chaotic
This is a space for adults who know themselves. The energy here should feel like a very good dinner party that happens to have a play space downstairs — unhurried, comfortable, and alive. We are not in a rush. There is no pressure to do anything, be anywhere, or impress anyone.
Inclusive, Not Cliquey
Everyone arrived here through the same door. Whether you are attending your first event or your fifteenth, whether you came as a couple or solo, whether you know ten people in the room or none — you belong here equally. No one should feel like an outsider in this space.
Playful, Not Predatory
Flirtation, chemistry, and playfulness are welcome and wonderful. Pursuit, pressure, and entitlement are not. There is a meaningful difference between expressing interest and creating pressure. Know the difference and stay firmly on the right side of it.
"The quality of this space is determined entirely by the people in it. You are not a guest here. You are a co-creator of this experience."
The Ember House — founding principle
Section 02
How We Treat Each Other

These are the behaviours that define the culture of The Ember House. They apply in every space — the lounge, the play space, the hallways, and outdoors.

1
Lead with Kindness
Greet people. Smile. Make space for someone who looks uncertain. Introduce yourself. You do not need to know someone to make them feel welcome. A warm acknowledgement costs nothing and means everything to someone who is new or nervous.
2
Respect Every Boundary — Including the Ones That Aren't Said Out Loud
Not every boundary gets stated explicitly. Body language, energy, and context communicate just as clearly as words. If someone seems disengaged, distracted, or closed off — that is a boundary. Honour it without requiring them to articulate it. Attentiveness is a form of respect.
3
Do Not Comment on What You Observe
What happens between consenting adults in this space is their business. Do not comment on, critique, or narrate other people's activities, bodies, choices, or relationships — to them or to anyone else. Curiosity is welcome. Commentary is not. Discretion extends inward, not just outward.
4
Rejection Is Not a Conversation
When someone declines an invitation — to talk, to play, to connect — that answer is complete. It does not require explanation, justification, or negotiation. Accept it with grace, move on with warmth, and understand that how you respond to a no says everything about your character.
5
Look After Each Other
If you see someone who looks overwhelmed, isolated, or distressed — check in with them. You do not need to fix anything. A quiet "are you okay?" can make a profound difference. If you are concerned about someone's wellbeing, tell the operator or consent monitor. Looking after each other is everyone's job.
6
Leave People Better Than You Found Them
Every interaction is an opportunity. The person you spoke with for ten minutes at the bar, the couple you played beside, the stranger you helped find the bathroom — leave all of them with the impression that this community is exactly as warm, safe, and welcoming as they hoped it would be. Be the reason someone comes back.
Section 03
Shared Spaces & Mixed Groups

Retreat events bring together people from different friend groups, relationship structures, and experience levels. The social lounge and common areas are shared by everyone equally. Here is how we make that work.

The Social Lounge Is Common Ground

The upper level lounge is not reserved for any group or couple. It belongs to everyone at the event equally. If you arrive in a group of eight and take over the main seating area, you are limiting the space available to the other eight people who also paid to be here. Be spatially generous. Make room. Invite people in rather than closing the circle.

You Don't Have to Know Someone to Be Friendly

It is natural to spend most of your time with the people you came with. That is completely fine. But The Ember House is also a community space, and some of the people here may be attending alone or with a partner they don't know many others through. A brief, genuine acknowledgement of a stranger — a smile, a hello, a moment of eye contact — goes a long way.

You are not required to socialize with everyone. But you are expected to be warm to everyone. Those are not the same thing.

Different Groups, One Space

People at retreat events will have different experience levels, different relationship structures, different comfort zones, and different reasons for being here. Some guests will be seasoned community members. Others will be attending their first event of this kind. None of these differences create a hierarchy. Experience does not entitle anyone to more space, more access, or more consideration than anyone else. Meet every person where they are.

The Play Space — Shared Etiquette

The play space is available to all guests during open play periods. This means you may be playing alongside people you have never met. That requires a particular kind of social intelligence.

  • Give active scenes room. Do not hover close to a scene in progress. Give participants physical and psychological space to be fully present with each other.
  • Do not interrupt a scene. If you wish to join an ongoing scene, wait for a natural break and ask explicitly — do not simply insert yourself.
  • Wipe down equipment after use. This is a basic act of consideration for the next person. Supplies are provided throughout the space.
  • Be mindful of noise. This is a shared space. Sounds carry. Be aware of the impact of high-volume interaction on others who are nearby.
  • Aftercare happens here too. If you see a couple or group in aftercare — quiet, close, coming down from a scene — give them space and privacy. Do not approach unless invited.
Section 04
Privacy, Discretion & Why This Space Exists

This section is the most important one in this document. Please read it carefully.

How This Space Came to Exist

The Ember House exists because someone decided to build a space that should exist — a private, safe, purpose-built environment where consenting adults can explore freely, without judgment, without exposure, and without fear. It exists because the community it serves deserves better than improvised arrangements and unsuitable alternatives.

But spaces like this are fragile. They depend entirely on the discretion of everyone who uses them. A single careless conversation — at a dinner party, in a text message, on social media — can unravel everything that took courage and resources to build. Not just for the operator. For every guest who trusted this space with something deeply personal.

🔒
What Discretion Protects
Every guest here has a life outside these walls — a career, a family, a community, a reputation. They chose to come here because they trusted that what happens here stays here. When you protect this space with your silence, you are protecting every one of those lives. Discretion is not a rule imposed on you. It is an act of care for the people standing next to you.
⚠️
What Disclosure Destroys
Speaking about what you saw, who you saw, or what happened inside The Ember House — to anyone outside of it — does two things simultaneously. It exposes the private lives of people who trusted you with something intimate. And it puts the continued existence of this venue at risk. Once that trust is broken, this space cannot function. It cannot exist. There is no version of this story where disclosure is harmless.
What Discretion Looks Like in Practice
  • Do not identify this venue to anyone who is not already a confirmed guest — not the name, not the address, not the general area
  • Do not name or describe other guests to anyone outside this community, in any form, ever
  • Do not post about this venue on any social media platform, public or private, regardless of how vague you think you are being
  • Do not photograph or record anything or anyone on this property — this rule exists precisely because images cannot be unshared
  • Do not discuss specific activities or scenes you witnessed even within the community, unless you have the explicit consent of everyone involved
  • If someone recognizes you outside of this space and you are not certain they are comfortable being acknowledged in that context — follow their lead, not your own comfort
"What you witnessed here belongs to the people who lived it. It is not yours to carry out the door."
The Ember House — on discretion

The Stakes Are Real

A guest whose attendance here becomes known without their consent could face consequences in their professional life, their family relationships, their community standing. These are not hypothetical risks. They are the reason this community has always operated through private channels, word of mouth, and mutual trust.

The Ember House asks you to take those stakes seriously. Not because we are asking you to keep a secret for us — but because you are keeping a promise to every person in this room who trusted you enough to be here alongside them.

Protect this space. Protect each other. That is how we ensure this community continues to exist.

Section 06
A Final Note

This Space Is Rare. Treat It That Way.

Spaces like The Ember House exist because a small number of people decided to build something that should exist — and because the community that uses them understands what they have and protects it. A venue like this can only survive if every person in it takes responsibility for keeping it safe, warm, and worth coming back to.

You are part of that. Not just while you are here, but in the way you talk about this place (with discretion), the way you represent this community to others (with pride and care), and the way you treat the people you met here the next time you cross paths (with the same warmth you showed them the first night).

This community exists because people like you have protected it. Every time you leave here without saying a word about what happened inside, you make it possible for this space to open its doors again. Every time you treat a stranger here with genuine kindness, you make this community worth protecting.

Thank you for being here. Thank you for being the kind of person who reads a Code of Conduct and means it. Now go have a wonderful time.
